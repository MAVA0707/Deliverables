# Lab Summary — Relevance Scoring and Rerankers

Reranking helped most when embedding similarity alone returned topically adjacent but not precisely relevant chunks — particularly for narrow legal queries such as "What penalties exist for non-compliance?" where many EU AI Act chunks mention compliance in passing but only a small number contain the actual fine amounts and infringement thresholds. In those cases, the cross-encoder reranker reliably promoted the precise chunk to position 1, while embedding-only search often ranked a more generic compliance passage first. LLM-based scoring added a useful interpretability layer (the `reason` field explains *why* a chunk was scored as it was), making it easier to debug retrieval failures, but at roughly 6–10× the cost and latency of the cross-encoder. I would recommend using a cross-encoder reranker whenever the domain demands high precision (legal, medical, regulatory compliance) and the corpus mixes document types with different writing styles — as is the case here with formal regulatory text alongside conversational podcast transcripts — because the cross-encoder's joint query–document attention is far better at distinguishing "directly answers the question" from "related but tangential" than cosine similarity between independent embeddings. LLM reranking is worth the extra cost only when you also need an explainability signal (e.g., to surface a reason to the end user or to audit retrieval quality during development), but for production pipelines at scale the cross-encoder offers the best accuracy-to-cost trade-off.
