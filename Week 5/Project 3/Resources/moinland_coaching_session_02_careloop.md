**Topic:** Leadership alignment during early team growth

## Summary

CareLoop Labs is developing workflow software for outpatient care providers. The founding team has grown from 3 to 14 people in five months and is starting to feel the strain of unclear ownership, slow internal decisions, and inconsistent communication.

Daniel’s coaching focused on helping the founders move from “everyone knows everything” to a more deliberate operating model. The team agreed that their biggest challenge is not talent, but the absence of explicit decision rights.

## Key themes

### 1. Founder roles must evolve before the team breaks

The founders are still behaving as if the company is a five-person team. Everyone is involved in product, hiring, customer calls, and investor updates.

Daniel reflected that this creates two hidden costs:

- Employees wait for founder consensus before acting.
- Founders confuse being informed with being needed.

The team agreed to define clearer domains.

### 2. Decision rights over job titles

Rather than rewriting job titles, Daniel suggested mapping decisions that currently create bottlenecks.

The founders identified five recurring decision categories:

1. Product roadmap tradeoffs
2. Customer implementation promises
3. Hiring process changes
4. Pricing exceptions
5. Investor communication

Each category now needs one accountable decision owner, one consulted founder, and a clear escalation threshold.

### 3. Psychological safety requires rhythm

Emilia shared that team members are increasingly bringing concerns privately instead of discussing them in team forums. Daniel framed this as an early warning signal.

The founders agreed to introduce a lightweight “tension review” every two weeks. The purpose is not therapy or complaint collection, but making operational friction discussable before it becomes personal.

## Decisions

- Priya will become accountable for product roadmap decisions.
- Max will become accountable for commercial decisions, including pricing exceptions.
- Emilia will become accountable for hiring process and team rituals.
- Investor updates will be drafted by Max and reviewed asynchronously by the others.
- The company will test a biweekly tension-review meeting for six weeks.

## Coaching prompts from Daniel

- “Where are you still acting like a founding team instead of a leadership team?”
- “Which decisions feel collaborative but are actually unclear?”
- “What does the team need permission to decide without you?”
- “Which meeting exists because trust is missing somewhere else?”

## Risks to watch

- Creating role clarity but not communicating it to the team.
- Using consensus as a way to avoid accountability.
- Allowing private founder disagreement to leak into team ambiguity.
- Treating culture as a values exercise instead of a system of repeated behaviors.
