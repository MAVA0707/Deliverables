# Coaching Session Notes — SignalStack

## Session details

**Date:** 2026-05-05  
**Coach:** Daniel Kraft, CEO, Moinland  
**Founders:** Lina Weber, Jonas Richter  
**Startup:** SignalStack  
**Stage:** Pre-seed  
**Topic:** Founder focus, early sales motion, and decision hygiene

## Summary

Lina and Jonas are building an AI-assisted alerting layer for industrial maintenance teams. The session focused on narrowing their initial customer profile, reducing context-switching between product and sales, and creating a weekly operating rhythm that helps them make decisions faster without creating founder burnout.

Daniel challenged the team to stop treating every promising conversation as equal. The founders agreed to concentrate on German mid-market manufacturers with existing maintenance software but weak escalation workflows. They also discussed how emotional pressure from investor conversations was causing them to overbuild instead of learning from customers.

## Key themes

### 1. Choose a smaller wedge

The founders had been speaking with manufacturers, logistics operators, and energy companies. Daniel pushed them to define a tighter wedge where pain, urgency, and access are strongest.

**Current pattern:**  
The team is customizing the story for every prospect, which makes feedback hard to compare.

**Coaching prompt:**  
“What would you stop doing this month if your only goal was to learn faster?”

**Resulting focus:**  
Mid-market manufacturing companies with 500–2,000 employees, distributed maintenance teams, and a high cost of unplanned downtime.

### 2. Separate learning from selling

The founders were blending discovery calls, product demos, and fundraising narrative practice into the same conversations.

Daniel suggested labeling each call with a single purpose before it happens:

- **Discovery:** understand workflow and pain
- **Validation:** test urgency and willingness to change
- **Sales:** secure next step with economic buyer
- **Reference:** learn from domain experts without selling

This should help the team avoid false signals.

### 3. Protect founder energy

Lina described feeling responsible for product, hiring, fundraising, and customer success at once. Jonas said he was “always busy but unsure what actually moved the company forward.”

Daniel framed this as a leadership-design issue rather than a personal productivity issue. The founders agreed to establish a weekly 45-minute founder review focused on decisions, not status.

## Decisions

- SignalStack will pause outreach to logistics and energy prospects for the next four weeks.
- The next 15 customer conversations will target maintenance leaders in German mid-market manufacturing.
- Every prospect call will be tagged as discovery, validation, sales, or reference before it is booked.
- Lina will own product-learning synthesis; Jonas will own pipeline quality and next-step discipline.
- The founders will run a Friday founder review using the same five-question template each week.

## Founder review template

1. What did we learn this week that changes our assumptions?
2. Which decision are we avoiding?
3. What should we stop doing next week?
4. Where did founder energy leak unnecessarily?
5. What is the single most important outcome for next week?

## Risks to watch

- Mistaking polite interest for urgency.
- Letting investor feedback pull the team away from customer evidence.
- Building integrations before a repeatable buyer pain is proven.
- Founder fatigue disguised as “hustle.”
