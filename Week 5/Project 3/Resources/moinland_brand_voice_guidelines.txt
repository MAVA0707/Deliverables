MOINLAND · BRAND VOICE GUIDELINES
For founders. By founders.

A practical guide for content writers to capture Daniel Kraft's voice —
built from his published blog posts, LinkedIn content, and brand philosophy.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHO WE ARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE BRAND IN ONE SENTENCE
Moinland is a founder-to-founder coaching company: no magic, no hacks — just
hard-won practical wisdom from 500+ coaching relationships, delivered with
radical honesty and genuine care.

DANIEL'S PERSPECTIVE SHAPES EVERYTHING
Daniel is a 5x founder with IPOs, pivots, and exits behind him — now a coach.
He writes as the person who has been in the room, made the mistakes, and found
the clarity. Content must carry that credibility. We never write as outside
observers of startup life. We write from inside it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE 5 VOICE PILLARS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. RADICALLY DIRECT
   No padding. No softening. Say the real thing first.

2. EARNED EMPATHY
   We've been stuck too. We know the feeling — no performance.

3. PRACTICAL OVER THEORY
   Every idea lands with a framework, question, or action.

4. PROVOCATIVE CLARITY
   We challenge assumptions. Polite but pointed.

5. FOUNDER-FIRST
   The founder is the hero. We are the guide.

TONE IN PRACTICE — DO THIS, NOT THAT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DO:
  "Build, ship, sell, repeat. Everything else is a waste of your time."

DON'T:
  "It's important to prioritize your core business activities when possible."

---

DO:
  "Your brain isn't tired from working — it's tired from deciding alone."

DON'T:
  "Decision fatigue is a common challenge faced by modern executives."

---

DO:
  "Leadership is a toolbox, not a personality type. Who you are matters less
   than what you use."

DON'T:
  "Great leaders come in many different shapes and styles — find the approach
   that works for you!"

  "Dopamine has a nasty tendency not to last. The startup path is paved with

   goals."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO STRUCTURE A MOINLAND POST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 1 — HOOK WITH A REAL FOUNDER TENSION
Name the uncomfortable truth or the moment of stuckness immediately. No
warm-up. "When founders reach the end of their runway…" not "Today we'll
discuss financial management."

STEP 2 — VALIDATE THE EXPERIENCE (BRIEFLY)
One or two sentences that say "we've been there." Use "we" sparingly and
authentically. This isn't a therapy session — it's acknowledgment before
insight.

STEP 3 — REFRAME OR CHALLENGE
Offer the counter-intuitive perspective. "Leadership is a toolbox, not a
personality trait." This is the intellectual core — make it quotable and
crisp.

STEP 4 — GIVE A PRACTICAL FRAMEWORK OR QUESTIONS
Daniel always lands abstractions with tools: a list of 2–4 questions, a
decision checklist, or a concrete action. "Here are three questions we use
in coaching…"

STEP 5 — END WITH AN INVITATION, NOT A CTA
Close with a question to the reader or a reflection prompt — not a sales
pitch. "What's your favorite question when you're stuck?" Engagement over
conversion.

LANGUAGE — USE THESE, AVOID THOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WORDS AND PHRASES THAT FIT THE VOICE:
  clarity · real-life experience · practical · craft · focus · no-bullshit ·
  supportive · stuck · dare · honest · transformative · mental fitness ·
  founder journey · decision architecture · sparring partner

WORDS THAT BREAK THE VOICE:
  synergy · leverage · thought leadership · disruptive · game-changer ·
  empowering solutions · holistic approach · best practices · paradigm shift ·
  unlock your potential · revolutionary · seamlessly


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FORMATTING & STYLE RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SHORT SENTENCES. SHORT PARAGRAPHS.
Daniel writes in punchy rhythms. One idea per paragraph. Use white space
aggressively. A line break can be more powerful than a transition sentence.

WRITE "WE" AND "YOU" — ALMOST NEVER "ONE"
The relationship is between us (Moinland, having been founders) and you (the
reader, currently a founder). Avoid passive voice and impersonal constructions.

USE DATA TO ANCHOR, NOT TO DECORATE
Daniel cites a stat only when it's striking and relevant: "72% of founders
feel their mental health is affected." No data padding. One good number beats
five mediocre ones.

EMOJIS: CONTEXTUAL, SPARING, NEVER DECORATIVE
On LinkedIn, Daniel uses 1–3 emojis as section markers or emotional signposts.
Never use emojis in long-form blog posts. Never use them as generic decoration.

DON'T WRITE SELF-HELP. WRITE COACHING.
There's a difference. Self-help is cheerful, vague, and aspirational. Coaching
is direct, honest, and occasionally uncomfortable. If a sentence could appear
in a generic motivational post, rewrite it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OUR AUDIENCE — WRITE FOR THIS PERSON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRIMARY READER: THE ACTIVE FOUNDER / CEO
They're 2–10 years in. They've got traction, a team, real pressure. They're
smart and skeptical — they've read all the startup blogs and are tired of
surface-level takes. They want someone who has actually done it to tell them
something true and useful. They don't have time for fluff.

SECONDARY READER: THE LEADERSHIP TEAM MEMBER
A co-founder, CPO, or VP who is growing into a leadership role. Often the one
who brings Daniel's content to their CEO. Write so both of them nod in
recognition.
