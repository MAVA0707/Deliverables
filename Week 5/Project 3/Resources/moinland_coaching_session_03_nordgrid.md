## Session details

**Date:** 2026-05-18  
**Coach:** Daniel Kraft, CEO, Moinland  
**Founders:** Sara Lindholm, Tom Becker  
**Startup:** NordGrid Analytics  
**Stage:** Series A preparation  
**Topic:** Fundraising narrative, founder resilience, and strategic focus

## Summary

NordGrid Analytics provides forecasting tools for renewable-energy asset operators. The company has strong technical proof points but is struggling to translate product depth into a simple fundraising story. Sara and Tom also reported fatigue after months of investor meetings, customer escalations, and hiring pressure.

Daniel helped the founders distinguish between a company narrative for investors and an operating narrative for the team. The session ended with a clearer fundraising storyline, a decision to simplify the pitch around one expansion thesis, and a commitment to protect founder recovery time during the next fundraising sprint.

## Key themes

### 1. The pitch is too technically correct

Sara and Tom’s deck explains forecasting accuracy, grid volatility, asset classes, integrations, and model architecture. Daniel observed that the story is credible but cognitively heavy.

The recommended narrative sequence:

1. Energy assets are becoming harder to operate profitably.
2. Operators are losing margin because forecasts are fragmented.
3. NordGrid gives operators one decision layer for forecast-driven action.
4. The first wedge is wind and solar portfolio optimization.
5. The long-term opportunity is the operating system for distributed energy decisions.

### 2. Do not fundraise from exhaustion

The founders described fundraising as “a second company running on top of the first one.” Daniel challenged them to design the process with more constraints.

The founders agreed to:

- Limit investor meetings to three days per week.
- Keep Mondays free for internal leadership and customer priorities.
- Use Friday afternoons only for investor follow-up and reflection.
- Avoid rewriting the deck after every single investor comment.

### 3. One expansion thesis

The founders had been presenting three possible expansion paths: new geographies, new asset classes, and enterprise platform expansion. Daniel encouraged them to choose one as the primary Series A thesis.

They selected **asset-class expansion** as the main thesis, with geography treated as a secondary growth lever.

## Decisions

- The Series A narrative will focus on becoming the decision layer for renewable asset operators.
- Asset-class expansion will be the primary growth thesis.
- The pitch deck will be reduced from 22 slides to 12 core slides plus appendix.
- Investor meetings will be batched Tuesday through Thursday.
- Sara will lead the investor story; Tom will lead technical diligence and customer evidence.

## Draft investor narrative

NordGrid helps renewable-energy operators protect margin in increasingly volatile energy markets. As portfolios become more distributed and weather-dependent, operational decisions are only as good as the forecasts behind them. NordGrid turns fragmented forecasting inputs into one decision layer for asset operators, starting with wind and solar portfolio optimization and expanding across asset classes over time.

## Coaching prompts from Daniel

- “What is the simplest version of the company that is still true?”
- “Which slide exists because you are afraid of being challenged?”
- “What would an investor need to believe before the technical details matter?”
- “How will you protect founder judgment during the sprint?”

## Risks to watch

- Over-optimizing the deck for the last investor conversation.
- Letting technical credibility crowd out strategic clarity.
- Treating every investor as equally relevant.
- Allowing fundraising fatigue to lower decision quality.
