POST #1 — BLOG POST
"Death Brings Clarity — Stop the Fake Work in Your Startup"
Published: August 2023 · moinland.com
================================================================================

When founders reach the end of their runway, we see a significant shift in
their priorities. Deals, costs, and cash become central, and strategy or mental
fitness often slip into the background. Survival mode is on.

This confrontation with the brutal truth of startup life can be a healthy
shock, but it shouldn't be a surprise. A startup is all about survival.

Successful founders share one quality: Focus.

They don't waste time on fake work but are laser-focused on what contributes
to their startup's success. Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI and former president of
YCombinator, summarized it well:

"The main problem is [...] instead of relentlessly focusing on building a
great product and growing, they focus on everything else."

Fake work is like sugar. It feels great, but before you know it, you can't
live without it.

A simple test: measure the results of the activities on your calendar.

  - Networking: How many real deals did you secure at your last event?
  - Marketing: How many meetings did you book with your latest social post?
  - Tool: What incremental cash flow did your latest tool or "hack" generate?

Building a startup is like racing toward a cliff while building a bridge with
blazing wildfires chasing behind you. Either you get the bridge in place in
time, or you will crash and die.

Crashing is neither good nor bad, just a simple fact of (startup) life.
It doesn't even define how good you are as a leader.

As the founder, you may not control the destination of your business,
but you control how to prioritize your time.

Build, ship, sell, repeat.
Everything else is a waste of your time.

POST #2 — BLOG POST
"Wasteland of Founder Energy"
Published: July 2023 · moinland.com
================================================================================

Building a successful business requires immense clarity and rigorous focus.
You don't achieve this by exhausting yourself on LinkedIn over the latest
AI trends or your "learning" about the newest startup event.

The startup path is paved with dopamine junkies. And yes, I know how FOMO
makes it hard to say "no" to all the tempting options to shine.

Here are a few things to consider to improve your ROI on your time and
mental energy:

PITCH EVENTS WITHOUT INVESTORS
The goal of a pitch is to secure funding for your startup. If no investor is
in the room, you waste your time. There is nothing wrong with some public dry
runs to keep the jitters at bay — but does that require committing a full day
of prep, travel, and lousy finger food?

ACCELERATORS WITHOUT EXPERIENCED FOUNDERS OR DOMAIN EXPERTS
An accelerator is about upgrading your chances through education, practice,
and networking. Make sure the people you work with have real-life startup
experience as a founder. If not, they're likely reading the same blogs and
books you're reading.

STARTUP EVENTS WITHOUT REAL VALUE
The community among founders is of great value. Should you visit Slush,
Bits & Brezel, SXSW, or OMR? If you have set appointments with your target
investors or scheduled meetings with fellow founders, go for it. Otherwise,
check if the entertainment value justifies the investment and lost productivity.

'SO WHAT' AWARDS
Being the startup of the year can be a morale booster for you and the team.
Yet, dopamine has a nasty tendency not to last very long. What is the goal of
the award you're applying to, and which customers or investors care? And no,
€500 or €5,000 prize money is not worth the effort.

Build, ship, sell, repeat.
Everything else is a waste of your time.

POST #3 — BLOG POST
"The Fundamentals of Positioning — Who Buys, What They Buy, and Why"
Published: 2022 · moinland.com
================================================================================

As early founders, we often believe that everyone needs our product or
service. That is rarely the case.

Success comes with a clear understanding of who is buying, what they are
buying, and why. Not everyone can and will be your customer. And that is a
good thing — because it gives you the possibility to be the best in your
specific market. And you need to be the best.

The 3W — Who, What, Why — are the essence of your business, the driver
behind your startup. Before you design your logo or even think about your
pitch deck, you should answer them.

WHO IS YOUR CUSTOMER?

Almost every founder we talk to can articulate the 3W in the abstract, but
once we dig deeper, you'll be surprised how many founders don't have a clear
answer — even at a larger scale. Try it yourself: take a minute and write
down your answers.

The difference is in the language. Look at two examples from our community:

  Staffbase: "We reach all our employees with our communication."
  BeeKeeper: "We connect with our unconnected field workers."

All customers purchased the same type of product — but all of them had a
different way to express it. Only if you immerse yourself in the language of
your customers will you be able to articulate the What.

Stop thinking from the inside out and communicating why your product is so
great. Instead, look from the outside in: What do they need your product to
do for them? How does it fit into their lives? What problem does it solve?

WHY DO THEY BUY?

This is probably the most difficult question to answer, but the most important
one. Purchase decisions depend on who you are and what you are trying to
achieve — and are often not rational but emotional.

  Staffbase: "Our company goes through a lot of change and we need to support
  that change across the organization. We need to take control of the employee
  communication or they'll find external sources."

  BeeKeeper: "Most of our value is generated by employees in the field but
  they are disconnected from the company. They are the core of our business
  and we need to take care of them."

Having your 3W in order clears the path. It provides a framework to set goals,
empower your people, and make sure you always have enough cash in the bank.

POST #4 — BLOG POST
"Experience Matters in Startup Success"
Published: September 2022 · moinland.com
================================================================================

The data is clear: experience correlates with success.

The majority of founders are first-time founders. And the data confirms that
the experience of both the founders and founder teams correlates with exits.

Ali Tamaseb, partner at DCVC, a San Francisco-based VC firm, researched over
30,000 data points for his book "Super Founders," examining why some companies
turn into billion-dollar companies while most others don't.

He found that repeat founders were more likely to reach unicorn status. He
points out that "practice makes perfect, even in entrepreneurship" — building
anything, even a side project or non-profit, correlates with later success.

This observation resonates with our own experiences at Moinland.

Experience matters. Most challenges our startup CEOs face are not big
roadblocks that require new mystical super powers. Often it's the dialog with
somebody who has been there before that makes the difference.

That's why every coach at Moinland has been a founder and startup leader.
You can feel this shared experience in every session, workshop, or camp.

Tapping into your local startup community, joining a Slack channel, or simply
meeting fellow founders are often great first steps. But when you're ready to
go deeper — that's where coaching comes in.

The chance of a successful exit increases by approximately 200% for teams with
multiple startup experience. That gap doesn't close by reading more blogs.
It closes by doing — or by learning from someone who already has.

POST #5 — LINKEDIN POST
"Great Questions — The Most Powerful Tool in Your Founder Toolbox"
Published: 2024 · LinkedIn (@danielkraft)
================================================================================

What's your favorite question?

As founders, we always face situations when we feel stuck. Everything seems
to go wrong, dead ends everywhere, energy is low… you get the picture.

Our can-do mind urges us to action. Sometimes that is the right thing to do.
More often than not it is to make us feel in control.

That is the moment when a great question can bring clarity and make a real
difference.

Here are three of my favorite questions, which some of you might recognize
from our coaching:

  "50,000 people cheering you on at your user group — what excited them?"

  "What of that will still be important tomorrow?"

  "What's underneath that is not being said?"

Questions are one of the most important tools to gain new insights.
Great questions make us think, reach deeper, and discover.

They are also the foundation of great coaching. Not because we have all the
answers — but because the right question in the right moment can unlock what
you already know but haven't yet been able to see.

What is your favorite question?
