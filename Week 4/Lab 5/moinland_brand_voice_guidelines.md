# Moinland · Brand Voice Guidelines

> **For founders. By founders.**
> A practical guide for content writers to capture Daniel Kraft's voice — built from his published blog posts, LinkedIn content, and brand philosophy.

---

## Who We Are

### The brand in one sentence

Moinland is a founder-to-founder coaching company: no magic, no hacks — just hard-won practical wisdom from 500+ coaching relationships, delivered with radical honesty and genuine care.

### Daniel's perspective shapes everything

Daniel is a 5x founder with IPOs, pivots, and exits behind him — now a coach. He writes as the person who has been in the room, made the mistakes, and found the clarity. Content must carry that credibility. We never write as outside observers of startup life. We write from inside it.

## The 5 Voice Pillars

| Pillar | Description |
|---|---|
| **Radically direct** | No padding. No softening. Say the real thing first. |
| **Earned empathy** | We've been stuck too. We know the feeling — no performance. |
| **Practical over theory** | Every idea lands with a framework, question, or action. |
| **Provocative clarity** | We challenge assumptions. Polite but pointed. |
| **Founder-first** | The founder is the hero. We are the guide. |

## How to Structure a Moinland Post

**Step 1 — Hook with a real founder tension**
Name the uncomfortable truth or the moment of stuckness immediately. No warm-up. "When founders reach the end of their runway…" not "Today we'll discuss financial management."

**Step 2 — Validate the experience (briefly)**
One or two sentences that say "we've been there." Use "we" sparingly and authentically. This isn't a therapy session — it's acknowledgment before insight.

**Step 3 — Reframe or challenge**
Offer the counter-intuitive perspective. "Leadership is a toolbox, not a personality trait." This is the intellectual core — make it quotable and crisp.

**Step 4 — Give a practical framework or questions**
Daniel always lands abstractions with tools: a list of 2–4 questions, a decision checklist, or a concrete action. "Here are three questions we use in coaching…"

**Step 5 — End with an invitation, not a CTA**
Close with a question to the reader or a reflection prompt — not a sales pitch. "What's your favorite question when you're stuck?" Engagement over conversion.

## Language — Use These, Avoid Those

### Words and phrases that fit the voice

`clarity` `real-life experience` `practical` `craft` `focus` `no-bullshit` `supportive` `stuck` `dare` `honest` `transformative` `mental fitness` `founder journey` `decision architecture` `sparring partner`

### Words that break the voice

~~synergy~~ ~~leverage~~ ~~thought leadership~~ ~~disruptive~~ ~~game-changer~~ ~~empowering solutions~~ ~~holistic approach~~ ~~best practices~~ ~~paradigm shift~~ ~~unlock your potential~~ ~~revolutionary~~ ~~seamlessly~~

## Formatting & Style Rules

**Short sentences. Short paragraphs.**
Daniel writes in punchy rhythms. One idea per paragraph. Use white space aggressively. A line break can be more powerful than a transition sentence.

**Write "we" and "you" — almost never "one"**
The relationship is between us (Moinland, having been founders) and you (the reader, currently a founder). Avoid passive voice and impersonal constructions.

**Use data to anchor, not to decorate**
Daniel cites a stat only when it's striking and relevant: "72% of founders feel their mental health is affected." No data padding. One good number beats five mediocre ones.

**Emojis: contextual, sparing, never decorative**
On LinkedIn, Daniel uses 1–3 emojis as section markers or emotional signposts. Never use emojis in long-form blog posts. Never use them as generic decoration (✅🚀💡).

**Don't write self-help. Write coaching.**
There's a difference. Self-help is cheerful, vague, and aspirational. Coaching is direct, honest, and occasionally uncomfortable. If a sentence could appear in a generic motivational post, rewrite it.

## Our Audience — Write for This Person

### Primary reader: the active founder / CEO

They're 2–10 years in. They've got traction, a team, real pressure. They're smart and skeptical — they've read all the startup blogs and are tired of surface-level takes. They want someone who has actually done it to tell them something true and useful. They don't have time for fluff.

### Secondary reader: the leadership team member

A co-founder, CPO, or VP who is growing into a leadership role. Often the one who brings Daniel's content to their CEO. Write so both of them nod in recognition.
